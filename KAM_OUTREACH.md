# Buyer Kit Sprint — KAM Outreach

Use this to test the offer with friends who sell into retailers, cafés, distributors, or other B2B buyers. It is a conversation starter, not a mass outreach sequence.

## The question to test

> When you meet a buyer, do you have sales material that makes the range, commercial facts, and next step genuinely easy to understand?

Avoid asking: “Do you need design help?”

That question produces vague answers. The Buyer Kit solves a specific commercial moment: a buyer needs enough confidence to agree to a tasting, list products, or start an order.

## First message — English

> Hey [name] — I’m developing a small, fixed-scope offer for product brands that sell through retailers.
>
> The idea is simple: many brands have good products but no genuinely useful way to show a buyer the range, pricing, EANs and next step. It becomes scattered PDFs, images attached to barcodes, and material that is hard to update.
>
> I call it a **Buyer Kit Sprint**: a clear sales pack with a buyer one-pager, range sheet, and a simple tasting/order leave-behind.
>
> I built a concept version here: [LINK TO /buyer-kit]
>
> Since you work in [KAM/sales], could I ask three quick questions about what actually helps in a buyer meeting? I’m trying to learn what is commercially useful, not just make something look good.

---

## The three questions

1. **What information do buyers ask for repeatedly that is usually missing, scattered, or badly presented?**
2. **At what point in the meeting or follow-up does a brand most often lose momentum?**
3. **If you could give a small product brand one physical or digital item before a buyer meeting, what would make your job easier?**

Optional fourth question:

4. **Would a fixed-price kit containing a one-pager, range sheet, barcode/EAN information and order-ready leave-behind be something you could introduce to a brand? What would make it an easy recommendation?**

## If they show interest

> I’m looking for one founding pilot where the scope stays tight: I take the existing product data, copy and imagery, then turn it into a buyer one-pager and range sheet that can be used in meetings and follow-ups. The founding pilot is **6,900 SEK excluding VAT**, with one revision round and finished PDF files included.
>
> If you know a brand that has a good product but weak sales material, I’d be happy to do a quick 20-minute fit call before proposing anything.

Do not discount immediately. A useful pilot is paid, tightly scoped, and gives the client something they can actually use.

---

## What to request from a prospective pilot

- Current sales material, even if it is messy
- Product list: flavours/SKUs, formats, case sizes, wholesale prices, EAN/barcodes
- Ingredients, allergen and shelf-life information where relevant
- Product photography and logo files
- A description of the ideal buyer and the desired next step
- Any existing order process, delivery terms, minimum order, or sample process

Never invent commercial claims, product facts, allergens, prices, barcodes, minimum orders, or lead times. The FJÄLLGLASS buyer-kit demo is explicitly fictional for this reason.

---

## What success looks like in the first month

- 5 KAM conversations
- 1 warm introduction to a product brand
- 1 paid Buyer Kit pilot
- A documented before/after case study, with the client’s written approval before publishing it

The goal is not volume. The goal is learning whether the material addresses a real, paid sales problem.
